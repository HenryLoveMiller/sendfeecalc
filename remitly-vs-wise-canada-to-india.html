The short answer

First transfer: Remitly's welcome rate delivered more than Wise on 1,000 CAD — and only because it is a promotional rate for new customers, capped at C$2,000.

Every transfer after that: Wise delivered more than Remitly's everyday rate on the same 1,000 CAD. If you are sending money regularly, this is the comparison that matters, and it is the one most write-ups leave out.

Neither is free. Wise charges a visible fee and uses the mid-market rate. Remitly charges little or no fee and builds most of its cost into the rate. Different place, same total.

Why the same amount buys different amounts of rupees

Two providers can both show a rate and both look cheap, because they charge in different currencies of effort.

Wise charges a separate fee. On 1,000 CAD it was 7.77 CAD, paid on top of the amount, and it uses the mid-market rate with no hidden markup. You can see the whole cost in one number. On the day we priced it, that cost left your family 617 INR short of a mid-market conversion.

Remitly charges little or no fee and puts the cost in the rate. On 1,000 CAD the transfer fee was 0 CAD, but the everyday rate sat 1.9% below mid-market. That difference is worth 1,287 INR on the same thousand — more than the fee it saved you. So a zero-fee quote is not automatically the cheapest quote.

Remitly's welcome rate inverts this, once. For a first transfer it paid above mid-market. That is a marketing cost the provider is choosing to absorb to win you, and it is not a rate you can plan on.

Questions people actually ask

Is Remitly cheaper than Wise for sending money from Canada to India?

On the day we priced it, it depends on whether it is your first transfer. Remitly's welcome rate for a new customer delivered more than Wise on 1,000 CAD, but Remitly's everyday rate delivered less. The welcome rate is a one-time offer for new customers, capped at 2,000 CAD. For every transfer after the first, we priced Wise ahead.

Why is the same amount worth different amounts of rupees at Remitly and Wise?

Two different things. Wise charges a separate transfer fee and uses the mid-market exchange rate, so its cost is visible. Remitly charges little or no fee and builds most of its margin into the rate instead, so its cost is hidden inside the rate. On 1,000 CAD we priced Wise's fee at 7.77 CAD and Remitly's at 0 CAD, but Remitly's rate sat further below mid-market.

Can I use Remitly's welcome rate for my regular transfers?

No. It applies to the first transfer only, to new customers only, one per customer, and only up to the stated cap. Plan on the everyday rate, and check what your own rate shows on the provider's site before you send.

Is a bank channel worth checking?

Yes. On the same day, ICICI Bank Canada's published Money2India rate beat the mid-market benchmark on 1,000 CAD — 765 INR ahead of it — with zero fees on a first transfer up to $1,000. That rate is also a first-transfer bonus rate, so the same caveat applies: check what applies to your second transfer, not just your first. We priced the bank channels against the same benchmark on the main calculator, and broke the bank channel down in full on the ICICI Bank Canada rate page.
