Taptap Send from Canada to India: the fee, and what your family actually receives

Taptap Send publishes one number for this corridor — a CA$1.99 fee — and leaves the rate in the app. Here is what that combination actually delivers, and why so many comparison pages quote Taptap as fee-free.

How this is built. The fee below is Taptap Send's own published fee, read from its pages on 6 November 2026. The rate is not published by Taptap Send — it comes from an independent rate tracker and is labelled as such wherever it appears. The mid-market reference is the European Central Bank reference rate for the business date the rate was read. Nothing here is estimated or averaged.

The short answer

Taptap Send charges CA$1.99 to send from Canada to India, and that fee is published flat. It is not a percentage and it does not fall as your transfer gets bigger — which is the part most comparison pages leave out.

On the rate we read, 1,000 CAD delivers about 67,615 INR. The mid-market reference for the same business date was 67.913, so the recipient gets roughly 298 INR less than a mid-market conversion — about 0.44%.

But most rate trackers quote Taptap Send at 0.00 CAD. We found trackers showing a zero transfer fee for Taptap, and one that states outright that Taptap Send showed no transfer fee in its records. Taptap Send's own fee table says CA$1.99 for Canada. On 1,000 CAD that gap is worth about 135 INR in the recipient's account, so it is not a rounding detail.

What Taptap Send's own pages actually say

These are the published statements, in their own words. They are the reason this page prints 1.99 CAD when other pages print zero.

"Our transfer fees are detailed here: Canada: $1.99 CAD — Europe (euro zone): €1.99 EUR — United Kingdom (UK): £1 GBP — United States (US): $1.99 USD"taptapsend.com — sending money to India, read 6 November 2026
"When sending to India, the following fee structure applies: … Canada — CA$1.99 … All other countries — No transfer fee, a small percentage is included in the exchange rate"help.taptapsend.com — how can I send money to India, page dated 25 September 2026
"Some transfers are fee-free. Others carry a small charge. It depends on where you are sending from and to, and how much you are sending. … When there is no transfer fee, we make a small margin on the exchange rate instead."help.taptapsend.com — what fees do I pay, page dated 20 August 2026

Three things follow from that text, and this page is built around them.

  • Canada to India is a 1.99 CAD fee, and it is the country's own line in the table. The "no transfer fee" case in Taptap Send's fee table is the row for "all other countries" — countries outside the ones it lists. Canada is not that row.
  • Taptap Send itself says the fee can depend on the amount and the method, and that only the app knows for sure. The published figure here is the flat country fee; the exact charge for your transfer is the one shown in the app before you confirm. We do not print a number we have not read.
  • Where there is no fee, the cost moves into the rate. That is the same mechanism that makes a zero-fee tracker reading misleading: a lower headline rate is not the same thing as a cheaper transfer.

The rate is a tracker reading, not a Taptap Send figure

This is the honest limit of what can be published about this corridor, and it is worth being blunt about it: Taptap Send does not publish a CAD to INR rate on its own website. It shows the rate in the app, on the screen before you confirm.

So the rate on this page — 67.75 INR per CAD — was read from an independent rate tracker on 4 November 2026, and it is labelled "tracker" wherever it appears on this site. The same tracker states plainly what its own reading means:

We are not calling the tracker wrong about the rate — it is reading the rate the same way we are. We are saying that its fee of 0.00 does not match the fee Taptap Send publishes for Canada to India. Where a provider publishes a figure and a tracker publishes a different one, this site follows the provider.

One more thing the tracker's own records are good for: on 100 CAD, the same rate with a 0.00 fee would deliver 6,775 INR instead of the 6,640 INR this page prints. If you are deciding on a small transfer, that difference decides it.

How the money gets there

Taptap Send's own guide to India sets out two ways to deliver, and both are worth knowing before you send.

Delivery methodTypical delivery timeWhat your recipient needs
Bank transfer (IFSC)Under 2 hours, though it may take up to 2 working days to appear in the accountAn active Indian bank account with IFSC code and account number
UPI (VPA)Within a few minutesA valid UPI address, often linked to their phone number
  • Limits on the India side: bank transfer up to 500,000 INR per transfer, UPI up to 200,000 INR per transfer, both depending on your verification level.
  • Limits on the Canada side: CA$3,500 per transfer, CA$9,500 daily, CA$30,000 over 14 days.
  • Payment from Canada: a Canadian debit card — Visa, Visa Electron, Mastercard or Maestro. Credit cards, prepaid cards, corporate cards and Interac are not accepted.
  • Your recipient does nothing. Funds arrive automatically; the recipient never has to go to a branch or tap an app.
  • You need a Canadian phone number and a Canadian debit card to open the account. Taptap Send in Quebec is only available to Quebec residents.

Questions people actually ask

How much does Taptap Send charge to send money from Canada to India?

Taptap Send's own fee table for transfers to India lists CA$1.99 when you send from Canada. That is the provider's published fee and it is a flat amount, not a percentage. Taptap Send's general fee page also notes that the exact fee always depends on the sending country, the destination country and the amount, and that the app shows the exact charge before you confirm.

Is Taptap Send really free to send from Canada?

No. Some rate trackers show Taptap Send with a transfer fee of 0.00 CAD, and one of them states that Taptap Send showed no transfer fee in its records. Taptap Send's own published fee table for Canada to India says CA$1.99. We follow the provider's own published figure, so this page uses 1.99 CAD. On 1,000 CAD that difference is worth about 135 INR in the recipient's account.

What does 1,000 CAD actually deliver through Taptap Send?

On the rate we read from an independent tracker on 4 November 2026, 67.75 INR per CAD, minus the published 1.99 CAD fee, 1,000 CAD delivers about 67,615 INR. The mid-market reference for the same business date was 67.913, so that is roughly 298 INR behind a mid-market conversion, about 0.44%.

Does the CA$1.99 fee matter more on a small transfer?

Yes, and by a lot. The fee is a flat amount, so it does not shrink with the transfer. It is 1.99% of a 100 CAD transfer, 0.20% of a 1,000 CAD transfer and 0.04% of a 5,000 CAD transfer. On 100 CAD the same rate leaves the recipient about 151 INR behind a mid-market conversion, against about 950 INR on 5,000 CAD, but as a percentage the small transfer is far worse off.

Where does the Taptap Send exchange rate come from?

Not from Taptap Send. Taptap Send does not publish a CAD to INR rate on its own site; it shows the rate in the app before you confirm. The rate on this page, 67.75 INR per CAD, was read from an independent rate tracker on 4 November 2026 and is labelled as a tracker reading, not a provider-published rate. The one thing Taptap Send does publish is its fee.

How long does a Taptap Send transfer from Canada to India take?

Taptap Send's own guide to sending to India lists bank transfer as under 2 hours, though the money may take up to 2 working days to appear in the recipient's account, and UPI as within a few minutes. Your recipient needs an Indian bank account with IFSC code and account number for a bank transfer, or a UPI address for UPI. Neither method requires the recipient to do anything.

How we work

  • Provider fees are copied from the provider's own published page. We do not adjust, average or estimate them. Where a tracker and a provider publish different figures for the same thing, we print the provider's.
  • Rates that a provider does not publish are labelled as tracker readings, and the page says so next to the number. A rate we could only read second-hand is not dressed up as a published figure.
  • Where a provider publishes a figure for a different day than the rest of the table, that row is measured against the mid-market reference for its own day, and says so.
  • We are not paid by the providers listed here. If referral links are added later, they will be marked as such at the point they appear, on this page, before you click.
