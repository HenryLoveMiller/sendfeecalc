Taptap Send from Canada to India: the fee, and what your family actually receives

Taptap Send's own pages do not agree on what it charges to send from Canada to India. Its fee table says CA$1.99. Its app listing says India is fee-free, always. The short answer

Taptap Send's own pages disagree about the fee, and the gap is about 135 INR on every transfer. Its India destination page and the India fee table in its help centre both list CA$1.99 for Canada, presented as a flat charge rather than a percentage. Its Google Play listing describes "zero transfer fees on key corridors" and lists "India: no fees, always", with the footnote "Exchange rate margin applies". Both are Taptap Send's own words, published by Taptap Send.

We price the first reading, CA$1.99, because it is the only one written per corridor and per sending country — a Canada-to-India row, not a corridor slogan — and because it is the one with a date on it. On 1,000 CAD at the rate we read, that delivers about 67,615 INR. If the fee-free reading is the one the app applies to you, the same amount delivers about 67,750 INR. On 100 CAD it is 6,640 against 6,775.

Against the mid-market reference for the same business date, 67.913, both readings lose. The fee explains 135 INR of that, and the rate explains the rest. A zero fee is not the same thing as a good rate, and a 1.99 fee is not the same thing as a bad one.

What a tracker showing 0.00 is doing is telling you which of Taptap Send's two statements it read. It is consistent with the app listing, not with the fee table, and no tracker can settle it for you: only the confirmation screen in your own app shows the fee and rate applied to your transfer.

What Taptap Send's own pages actually say

These are the published statements, in their own words — including the one that contradicts the other two. They are the reason this page shows both a CA$1.99 reading and a fee-free reading, rather than picking one and calling it the fee.

"Our transfer fees are detailed here: Canada: $1.99 CAD — Europe (euro zone): €1.99 EUR — United Kingdom (UK): £1 GBP — United States (US): $1.99 USD"taptapsend.com — sending money to India, read 6 November 2026
"When sending to India, the following fee structure applies: … Canada — CA$1.99 … All other countries — No transfer fee, a small percentage is included in the exchange rate"help.taptapsend.com — how can I send money to India, page dated 25 September 2026
"Zero transfer fees on key corridors — Nigeria: no fees, always* — India: no fees, always* — Mexico: no fees, always* — Brazil: no fees, always* … *Exchange rate margin applies."Google Play store listing for Taptap Send, Inc. — read 6 November 2026
"Some transfers are fee-free. Others carry a small charge. It depends on where you are sending from and to, and how much you are sending. … When there is no transfer fee, we make a small margin on the exchange rate instead."help.taptapsend.com — what fees do I pay, page dated 20 August 2026

Four things follow from that text, and this page is built around them.

  • The fee table puts Canada in its own row at CA$1.99. The "no transfer fee" line in that same table is the row for "all other countries" — countries outside the ones it lists. Canada is not that row.
  • The app listing says the opposite for this corridor: India is "no fees, always". Those are both Taptap Send's words, published by Taptap Send, and they cannot both describe the same transfer unless the fee table is out of date or the app listing is written loosely.
  • Taptap Send's own general fee page is the tie-break, and it puts the decision in the app. Fees depend on sending country, destination and amount, it says, and "the fee, if any, is always shown clearly in the app before you confirm." That is the only statement of the four that cannot be wrong about your transfer, because it is the one you can see applied.
  • Where there is no fee, the cost moves into the rate. The app listing's own footnote says so: "Exchange rate margin applies." That is why a zero fee is not the same claim as a good rate, and why a tracker reading of 0.00 does not tell you the transfer is cheap.

If the fee turns out to be zero for you, the first number is the one to expect. If it is 1.99, it is the second. Your app's confirmation screen settles it before you send — and that is the only figure that applies to your own transfer.

How the money gets there

Taptap Send's own guide to India sets out two ways to deliver, and both are worth knowing before you send.

Delivery methodTypical delivery timeWhat your recipient needs

UPI (VPA)Within a few minutesA valid UPI address, often linked to their phone number
  • Limits on the India side: bank transfer up to 500,000 INR per transfer, UPI up to 200,000 INR per transfer, both depending on your verification level.
  • Limits on the Canada side: CA$3,500 per transfer, CA$9,500 daily, CA$30,000 over 14 days.
  • Payment from Canada: a Canadian debit card — Visa, Visa Electron, Mastercard or Maestro. Credit cards, prepaid cards, corporate cards and Interac are not accepted.
  • Your recipient does nothing. Funds arrive automatically; the recipient never has to go to a branch or tap an app.
  • You need a Canadian phone number and a Canadian debit card to open the account. Taptap Send in Quebec is only available to Quebec residents.

What does 1,000 CAD actually deliver through Taptap Send?

On the rate we read from an independent tracker on 4 November 2026, 67.75 INR per CAD, minus the published 1.99 CAD fee, 1,000 CAD delivers about 67,615 INR. The mid-market reference for the same business date was 67.913, so that is roughly 298 INR behind a mid-market conversion, about 0.44%.

Does the CA$1.99 fee matter more on a small transfer?

Yes, and by a lot. The fee is a flat amount, so it does not shrink with the transfer. It is 1.99% of a 100 CAD transfer, 0.20% of a 1,000 CAD transfer and 0.04% of a 5,000 CAD transfer. On 100 CAD the same rate leaves the recipient about 151 INR behind a mid-market conversion, against about 950 INR on 5,000 CAD, but as a percentage the small transfer is far worse off.
