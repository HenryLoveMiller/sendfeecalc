Why the same rupees cost different amounts of CAD

Because you are buying a rate, not a currency. Each provider publishes its own rate, and the amount you must send is the rupees you need divided by that rate, plus whatever fee is taken off first. Two providers can quote rates a fraction of a percent apart and still differ by tens of dollars on a large transfer, once the fee is added.

The mid-market comparison is the honest way to read it. The benchmark converts your rupee target at the European Central Bank reference rate with no fee at all — the best possible case for mid-market. Where a channel’s figure sits below that, it beats mid-market on that day. Where it sits above, the gap is what the rate and fee actually cost you.

Wise is not priced at that amount because it publishes its transfer fee for 1,000 CAD only. A mid-market conversion of the same 50,000 INR needed 737.50 CAD at that day’s European Central Bank reference rate of 67.797, so the cheapest channel we price asked for less than mid-market and the most expensive asked for 14.27 CAD more.

Why do I need more CAD than the exchange rate suggests?

Because a quoted rate is not a payout. The amount you must send is the rupees you need divided by the rate you actually get, plus the transfer fee that is taken off before conversion. A channel can quote a rate close to mid-market and still cost more than mid-market once the fee is added, and a channel can quote a slightly better rate and win on the amount you send.

Which channel should I use?

We do not recommend one. We publish what each provider states, on the date we read it, and compare them like for like. Remitly’s welcome rate is a first-transfer promotion for new customers and its everyday rate is the one to plan on from your second transfer onwards; the bank offer we price is capped at the first 1,000 CAD. What this does not cover

  • Delivery times and speed options. Providers price express and economy differently and the published figures move; we leave them out rather than publish an average.
  • Payment method. The fee and rate we quote apply to the published page as read; some payment methods carry their own cost, which your own quote will show.
  • Your own rate. Providers set the rate you actually get by amount, payment method, corridor and eligibility. Use these figures to compare the shape of the cost, then check your quote on the provider’s site.
  • Anything after 3 November 2026. These are the published figures we read on that date; the sources page records exactly which page each one came from.

How we work

  • Provider rates and fees are copied from the provider’s own published pages. We do not adjust, average or estimate them.
  • Where an offer has a cap, we stop pricing at the cap instead of extrapolating a rate the provider has not published.
  • One provider appears more than once, because it publishes more than one rate. The rows are labelled.
  • Where a provider publishes no fee for your case, we print no number rather than a guess.
