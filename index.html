"Gap" is the difference between what that channel pays out and what a mid-market conversion would pay out on the same amount. Positive means the channel beats the mid-market (you receive more); negative means it loses to it. Each row is measured against the ECB reference for its own business date, so a figure published on an earlier day is compared with the benchmark from that day, not with today's. Why the headline rate lies

Every provider quotes a rate. Almost none of them quote the same rate. The number that matters is what lands in the recipient's account, and that is rate minus fee minus spread.

A 67.7 rate sounds good until a 7.77 CAD fee is taken off 1,000 CAD before conversion — that fee alone moves the payout by roughly 526 INR per thousand sent.

Questions people actually ask

Which channel is cheapest for a small amount?

Under 500 CAD, Remitly's published fee is 1.99 CAD. At or above 500 CAD it is 0 CAD. Bank channels often carry zero transfer fees but publish a wider spread against mid-market.

Does the bank channel ever win?

On the day recorded here, yes — but only just. ICICI Bank Canada's published Money2India rate beat the mid-market benchmark by 765 INR on 1,000 CAD, about 1%. Remitly's welcome rate also comes in above the benchmark, but that is a first-transfer promotion for new customers only, so it is not the number to plan on. Bank rates are often promotional for a first transfer and revert to a lower base rate afterwards — check what applies to your second transfer, not just your first.

Is a first-transfer promo rate the real rate?

No. A welcome rate is a one-time offer, capped at an amount, and for new customers only. Treat it as the exception, and the published everyday rate as the number to plan on.

How we work

  • Provider rates and fees are copied from the provider's own published page. We do not adjust, average or estimate them.
  • The mid-market benchmark in the table is the European Central Bank reference rate for the same business date as the provider rates, read from a dated endpoint. The live card at the top shows the latest published reference rate and is labelled with its own date — the two figures are not interchangeable.
  • One provider on this page appears more than once, because it publishes more than one rate. The rows are labelled.
  • Where a provider publishes a figure for a different day than the rest of the table, that row is measured against the mid-market reference for its own day, and says so. A gap always compares like with like.
  • Where a provider publishes no figure for your case — a second transfer, or an amount above its stated cap — we print no number rather than a guess. Those rows are marked "not priced".
  • We are not paid by the providers listed here. If referral links are added later, they will be marked as such at the point they appear, on this page, before you click.
